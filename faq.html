Got Questions?

Common Inquiries Answered

Find quick answers about material composition, pricing, shipping, custom casting options, and structural safety parameters.

Are fly ash bricks stronger than traditional red clay bricks?

Yes. While local red clay bricks have a compressive strength ranging between 3.5 to 5 N/mm², Meenakshi Flyash Bricks Fly Ash Bricks possess a compressive strength greater than 10 to 12.5 N/mm². This ensures they safely bear high structural load requirements and resist cracking under pressure.

Do fly ash bricks prevent wall dampness?

Yes. Clay bricks have high water absorption (typically 15% to 20%), which draws moisture and triggers salt peeling. Meenakshi Flyash Bricks Fly Ash blocks maintain water absorption under 8% to 10%, effectively preventing damp walls and efflorescence (salt weathering) from destroying paint.

Do these bricks qualify for LEED green rating points?

Absolutely. Meenakshi Flyash Bricks are composed of up to 70% industrial byproduct (fly ash) and do not deplete precious agricultural topsoil. Using them qualifies your construction design for maximum LEED and IGBC green rating points, supporting carbon footprint reduction guidelines.

Can you manufacture custom sizes or crushing strengths?

Yes. For large commercial or industrial projects exceeding 100,000 units, we can formulate custom mix designs to deliver specific crushing strengths (like heavy-duty 15 N/mm² or 20 N/mm²) or modify dimensional molds. Please consult with our engineering team for feasibility.

What is the minimum order quantity (MOQ) and lead time?

Our standard Minimum Order Quantity is 5,000 bricks for local delivery. Deliveries are typically dispatched within 48 to 72 hours from confirmation, depending on stock yard availability. Custom curing orders can take 10 to 14 days.

Do they save building mortar and plaster costs?

Yes, significantly. Because fly ash bricks are hydraulically pressed in metal molds, they have exact dimensions (±1mm). This uniformity reduces layout errors, requires less joint mortar (saving up to 20%), and results in flat surfaces that require thin plaster coats.
